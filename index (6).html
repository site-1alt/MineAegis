The Crisis

Invisible dangers become disasters.

Underground mines are complex environments where danger can rise silently. A miner may not see poisonous gas, fire may spread quickly, and a fall may go unnoticed.

☠️

Toxic Gas Exposure

Harmful gases can collect in low-ventilation zones and cause dizziness, unconsciousness, or death.

🔥

Fire & Methane Risk

Flammable gases and sparks can create sudden fire or explosion hazards underground.

🚨

Delayed Rescue

If a miner falls or gets trapped, every minute matters. Delayed communication can cost lives.

Real Incidents

Built from real-world mining risks.

Our project is inspired by mining accidents where earlier detection and faster communication could have improved rescue response.

Meghalaya Mine Tragedies

Several mine incidents in Meghalaya have shown how dangerous unmonitored underground mining can be, especially when miners become trapped and rescue teams lack live information.

Jharkhand Coal Belt Accidents

Coal regions have witnessed collapses, gas exposure, and fire-related accidents, proving the need for wearable safety technology and command-center monitoring.

Global Underground Mine Fires

Many global mine disasters have involved methane buildup, oxygen shortage, and poor communication. MineAegis directly targets these problems.

Our Solution

Helmet + Sensors + LoRa + Command Center.

A smart wearable system that protects miners locally and informs supervisors remotely.

1. Toxic Gas Detection

MQ gas sensor monitors air quality and alerts when dangerous gas levels rise.

2. Fire Detection

Flame sensor detects fire signs and sends a critical warning to the dashboard.

3. Fall Detection

Tilt/fall sensor detects sudden impact or abnormal helmet position.

4. Automatic Torch

LDR sensor turns on a high-power LED torch when darkness is detected.

5. Rescue Alert

If a fall is detected and no recovery happens, the supervisor receives a rescue alert.

    Questions

    What people ask about MineAegis.

    How far can the LoRa link reach?

    Open-air LoRa can cover kilometers, but tunnels block and bend signals. Real range depends on the mine layout, and long or winding galleries may need relay nodes.

    Are the sensors accurate enough for certified gas monitoring?

    No. MQ sensors give a rough reading and need calibration. This prototype shows the concept; a deployed system would use certified methane and CO detectors.

    What happens when a miner falls?

    The tilt sensor flags the fall. If the helmet does not return to a normal position, the supervisor dashboard raises a rescue alert.

    Does the helmet work without the command center?

    Yes. The buzzer and torch run on the helmet, so the miner is warned locally even if the radio link drops.
